# Multi-Part Efforts: Sibling Folders

Propose sibling tasks when an effort has delivery seams that can land and be verified independently (`./decomposition.md`). Each confirmed sibling has its own context, goals, plan, result, optional ticket, and lifecycle (`./task-layout.md`). Goal count or file count alone does not require a split. A tightly coupled fix stays in one folder.

When the user explicitly requests one task, keep one folder and one plan. Use the useful seams to bound its steps by verifiable outcomes, dependencies, and checkpoints.

When parts have a blocking order, the `NN-` folder prefixes give that order:

```
.agents/tasks/01-schema/
.agents/tasks/02-api/
.agents/tasks/03-ui/
```

A plan step that waits on a sibling names that sibling, or one of its steps, on its `**Depends on:**` line (`../templates/plan.md`). Name the sibling by its folder name, written `<folder>` or `<folder> Step <n>`, such as `01-schema Step 2`.

`taskCitation` and `stepRefsIn` in `scripts/task-state.ts` carry this citation form, a sanctioned copy per `references/maintenance/repository.md` § *Consumer lists*.

A citation of a sibling is met once that sibling's plan is `done`. A citation of one of its steps is met once that step is checked. Read the plan's status and steps from a read-only task-state run (`../scripts/task-state.md`; `<kit-root>`: `./task-store.md` § *Resolving `<kit-root>`*) on the sibling's folder in the same parent or under `<parent>/Archive/`. A `done` sibling awaits reopening while that run's `nextPendingStep` is not null. It also awaits reopening while that run's `currentState` **Next:** begins with the reopening marker (§ *Reopening marker*). A citation of such a sibling stays unmet, awaiting reopening of the sibling task. Once the plan is `done`, a step citation is met through `done` only when that run's `steps` list holds the cited step and shows it checked. When that list shows the cited step unchecked, the step citation stays unmet, awaiting reopening of the sibling task. When that list lacks the cited step, the step citation stays unmet, because the cited step is missing from the sibling's `done` plan. Check for both unmet outcomes before any commit record or merge proof. Such a citation takes neither. A citation of either form stays unmet when the helper is unavailable, the run exits non-zero, or neither place holds the sibling's folder. The recorded-done rule below is the exception: it can meet a citation of a sibling without a plan, whose run exits 1. When the sibling's `result.md` Current state `**Pointers:**` record a branch as `` branch `<branch>` ``, a citation of the sibling stays unmet until that branch is merged. That run's `currentState` holds those Pointers. The same branch requirement covers a step citation met through `done` and one whose branch is marked `(removed …)`. Under a live branch, a step citation met by its checked step before the plan is `done` instead needs its commit record's commit merged.

For both citation paths below, consider only the latest `Final implementation commit` record in the sibling's `result.md`. It qualifies only when no later checkpoint or final gate record shows `failed`, `not run`, or an unsettled hand-over. For a step citation, that Final record must also follow the cited step's latest result section. When finding that section, skip sections whose `**Executed:**` line reads `verification-only continuation`. Never use an earlier Final record to satisfy either path.

When the sibling's plan is `done` and its Pointers record a live branch, require the qualifying latest Final record. This applies to citations of the sibling and step citations met through `done`. An entry marked `(removed …)` is not live. Check the record before any merge proof. Its `**Commit:**` line must name a landed commit, written `` SHA `<sha>` ``, a settled hand-over, or `none — no task changes` (`./task-delivery.md` § *Checkpoint and final commits*). Read that line from `result.md` itself. Without such a record, the citation stays unmet, awaiting the sibling task's final commit, and takes no merge check. If the sibling's work was committed by hand, tell the user to append `## Final implementation commit — YYYY-MM-DD` to its `result.md`. Its `**Commit:**` line names `` SHA `<sha>` ``. Every accepted record proceeds to the merge proofs, including `none — no task changes`.

A step citation can be met by its checked step while the sibling's plan is not `done`. With a recorded branch, such a citation also needs a commit record, checked before the merge proofs. The sibling's `result.md` must hold a qualifying `Checkpoint after Step <m>` record or the qualifying latest Final record. Step `<m>` must be the cited step or a later step in the sibling plan's step order. Read that order from the task-state run's `steps` list (`../scripts/task-state.md`). For each such step, consider only the latest `Checkpoint after Step <m>` record. That record must follow the cited step's latest result section, skipping sections whose `**Executed:**` line reads `verification-only continuation`. It qualifies only when no later checkpoint or final gate record shows `failed`, `not run`, or an unsettled hand-over. Count a later checkpoint record only when its step is the cited step or a later step. The record's gate must have passed. Its `**Commit:**` line must name a landed commit, written `` SHA `<sha>` ``, a settled hand-over, or `none — no task changes` (`./task-delivery.md` § *Checkpoint and final commits*). Read that line from `result.md` itself, since task-state's output omits it. Without such a record, the citation stays unmet, awaiting commit of that step, and takes no merge check. If that work was committed by hand, tell the user to append `## Checkpoint after Step <m>` to the sibling's `result.md`. Record the passed gate and name `` SHA `<sha>` `` on its `**Commit:**` line. Under a branch marked `(removed …)`, apply the merge proofs to every accepted commit value. Under a live branch, prove the record's commit instead of the branch tip, as described after the proofs. Run the merge checks read-only in the repository that `./task-delivery.md` § *Branch and worktree creation* → **Which repository** picks for the sibling. Resolve `<default-branch>` under **Resolving `<default-branch>`** in that section. Test the local default branch's current head, where a new task branch starts, without fetching. Accept the first successful proof in this order:

1. **Ancestry:** `./task-delivery-edges.md` § *Removal* → **Ancestry** on `<branch>`, with `<default-branch>` as `<target>`.
2. **Merged PR:** `gh pr view <branch> --json number,state,mergeCommit,headRefOid` reports state `MERGED`. Require `git merge-base --is-ancestor <mergeCommit.oid> <default-branch>` to exit 0. When `<branch>` exists locally, also pass the head check on `<branch>` (`./task-delivery-edges.md` § *Removal* → **Head check**), so the merged PR holds the branch's current tip. This proof also applies when the branch no longer exists locally, without that head check.
3. **Patch equivalence:** `./task-delivery-edges.md` § *Removal* → **Patch equivalence** on `<branch>`, with `<default-branch>` as `<target>`.

Missing `gh`, an unresolved repository, failed reads, and unresolved branches provide no proof. A branch marked `(removed …)` takes the merged-PR proof without its head check. When its marker carries `merged at <sha>`, first accept `git merge-base --is-ancestor <sha> <default-branch>` exiting 0. Without `merged at`, when the merged-PR proof fails, prove the commit of the sibling's qualifying latest Final record instead. `<sha>` is that record's `` SHA `<sha>` `` or settled hand-over's SHA. When its `**Commit:**` line reads `none — no task changes`, take `<sha>` from an earlier record as the no-branch path below does. Run proofs 1 and 3, in order, substituting `<sha>` for `<branch>`. If neither succeeds, the citation stays unmet, awaiting merge of commit `<sha>`. In any other case where no proof succeeds, the citation stays unmet, awaiting merge of that branch. When proof 2 read state `MERGED` but `<mergeCommit.oid>` is missing locally or is not an ancestor of `<default-branch>`, that unmet citation awaits update of the local default branch instead. Apply that local-default-branch outcome also when the removed marker's `merged at <sha>` ancestry test fails and no later proof succeeds. When proof 2's merge-commit test passed but its head check failed on a missing PR head, that unmet citation instead awaits the PR head `<headRefOid>`, which is not available locally. Name it and the fetch the user needs under `./task-delivery-edges.md` § *Removal* → **Missing PR head**.

Under a live branch, a step citation met by its checked step proves one commit, `<sha>`, instead of the branch tip. When several records qualify, use the checkpoint record for the earliest step in the plan's step order, else the Final record. `<sha>` is that record's `` SHA `<sha>` `` or settled hand-over's SHA. When its `**Commit:**` line reads `none — no task changes`, take `<sha>` from an earlier record as the no-branch path below does. Without that earlier record, apply that path's awaiting-commit outcome, hand-edit guidance, and `**Depends on:**` revision guidance. Run the proofs in order, substituting `<sha>` for `<branch>` in proofs 1 and 3. Proof 2 keeps `gh pr view <branch>` and its merge-commit ancestry test. Its head check always runs on `<sha>`, so the merged PR holds that commit. If no proof succeeds, the citation stays unmet, awaiting merge of commit `<sha>`. Proof 2's local-default-branch and missing-PR-head outcomes above still apply to a `MERGED` read.

A branch entry records its base as `` branch `<branch>` (base `<base>`) `` (`./task-delivery.md` § *Branch and worktree creation*). A removed marker keeps that base as its last item, `` base `<base>` `` (`./task-delivery-edges.md` § *Removal*). Read a base only from these exact forms; other base text, such as a base followed by a SHA, leaves the base unread. A sibling branch takes the shared-base path when its entry reads the same `<base>` as the citing task's live branch entry, `<citing-branch>` below. That `<base>` must differ from `<default-branch>`. On that path, first run the branch, step-commit, and removed-branch tests above against the local `<base>` branch's current head, in place of `<default-branch>`. When none succeeds there, run them against `<default-branch>`. If neither run succeeds, take the `<base>` run's outcome. Its local-default-branch outcome awaits update of the local `<base>` branch instead. When the `<base>` run succeeds, the citing-branch tests below name `<base>` in place of the default branch in their outcomes. Every other sibling takes only the default-branch tests and outcomes above.

When the sibling's Pointers record no branch, read its `CONTEXT.md` `**Domain:**` header, defaulting to engineering. For an engineering sibling, resolve its repository through **Which repository** above. Skip commit-record and merge checks only for a non-engineering sibling or when that repository stays unresolved. Otherwise, require the same qualifying Final or checkpoint record as the corresponding recorded-branch citation path above. Apply that path's awaiting-commit outcome and hand-edit guidance when the record is missing. When several records qualify, choose one as the live-branch step commit proof above does. When the qualifying record's `**Commit:**` line reads `none — no task changes`, take the SHA to prove from an earlier record. Use the latest checkpoint or Final record before it in the sibling's `result.md` whose `**Commit:**` line names a commit. A named commit is `` SHA `<sha>` `` or a settled hand-over's SHA. That earlier record supplies only the SHA to prove, not the qualifying record. Without such an earlier record, apply the same outcome and hand-edit guidance as for a missing record. Also tell the user to revise the citing plan's `**Depends on:**` line through `plan-task` if the cited work produced no commit. For a recorded SHA, a settled hand-over's SHA, or a SHA taken from an earlier record, run proofs 1 and 3, in order, substituting `<sha>` for `<branch>`. Use the same sibling repository, default-branch resolution, and read-only checks without fetching. If neither proof succeeds, the citation stays unmet, awaiting merge of commit `<sha>`.

Also check the citing task's own `result.md` Current state `**Pointers:**` for a live `` branch `<branch>` `` entry. An entry marked `(removed …)` is not live. With no recorded live branch, apply the checkout rule below. Otherwise, call that branch `<citing-branch>` and keep the following tests read-only, without fetching. For a sibling branch that passed the merge check, repeat the ordered proofs above against `<citing-branch>` in place of `<default-branch>`. Accept the sibling tip's ancestry, the merged PR's merge-commit ancestry and head check, or patch equivalence to the citing branch. If none succeeds, the citation stays unmet, awaiting update of the citing branch from the default branch. When the repeated proof 2's merge-commit test passed but its head check failed on a missing PR head, apply the missing-PR-head outcome above instead. For a removed sibling branch that passed the merge check, first test its marker's `merged at <sha>`, when present. Accept `git merge-base --is-ancestor <sha> <citing-branch>` exiting 0. Otherwise use the merged-PR proof against `<citing-branch>`, without its head check. When a merged PR is found, require `git merge-base --is-ancestor <mergeCommit.oid> <citing-branch>` to exit 0. Missing `gh`, a failed read, no merged PR, or any other exit fails that proof. Without `merged at`, when that proof fails, run proofs 1 and 3, in order, against `<citing-branch>` on the Final record's `<sha>` that the removed-branch rule above takes. If no test succeeds, the citation stays unmet, awaiting update of the citing branch from the default branch.

For a sibling SHA that passed the merge check, repeat its proofs against `<citing-branch>` in place of `<default-branch>`. A step commit `<sha>` under a live branch takes this rule instead of the branch tests above and repeats proofs 1 to 3. Other SHAs repeat proofs 1 and 3. If none succeeds, apply the same unmet outcome awaiting update of the citing branch from the default branch. A repeated proof 2 takes the missing-PR-head outcome as the branch tests above do.

With no recorded live branch, `implement-task` §4 **Source** repeats those branch or SHA tests against the selected current checkout's `HEAD` instead of `<citing-branch>`. Run them read-only in that checkout, without fetching. If none succeeds, the citation stays unmet, awaiting update of the current checkout. The `start-group` brief and `implement-task` §1 check for `to-do`, `blocked`, and qualifying `in-review` plans take no further test beyond the default-branch proofs in this case.

A sibling without a plan in the same parent, not under `<parent>/Archive/`, meets a citation of itself or of one of its steps once it is recorded done. A done-record line in the parent's `GROUP_CONTEXT.md` qualifies under `./task-store.md` § *Shared group context*. Its own `CONTEXT.md` Open Questions can instead record its work as done. They can also record as done the ticket its Scope line in the parent's `GROUP_CONTEXT.md` names.

Siblings share no sibling-level context file or cross-folder links. Duplicate needed facts into each context when no applicable ancestor holds them. Within a registered root, cite group-wide constraints from `GROUP_CONTEXT.md` (`./task-store.md` § *Shared group context*). Name and source each inherited fact; every folder remains independently discoverable, movable, and archivable.

Keep one effort's siblings in one parent. Finished parts move under `<parent>/Archive/`; unstarted parts park under `<parent>/Backlog/` (`./task-archiving.md`, `./task-backlog.md`).

Choose and materialize cuts through `./decomposition.md` and `decompose-task`.

## Reopening marker

The reopening marker is the literal text `Reopen this done plan:`, written first after the `**Next:**` label in a task's `result.md` Current state. A writer that leaves a `done` plan holding new work, such as an added step or goal, begins that task's Next with the marker. After the marker, Next names the remaining work and the route that reopens the plan, `resume-task-reconcile` or `review-task-reconcile`. Without the marker, a Next that names either skill does not make a `done` plan await reopening.

A reader routes a `done` task awaiting reopening to `review-task-reconcile` when its Next begins with the marker and names only that skill. Every other such task routes to `resume-task-reconcile`.

## Repair marker

The repair marker is the literal text `Repair this in-review plan:`, placed in Current state Next as § *Reopening marker* places its marker. A writer that leaves an `in-review` plan meeting the goal-verdict condition of `in-review → executing` begins that task's Next with it. The writer does so only while the plan holds no unexecuted step (`./status-transitions.md` § *Downward reconciliation and upward advances*). After the marker, Next names by ID each goal that meets that condition and the route that repairs the plan `in-review → executing`, `resume-task-reconcile` or `review-task-reconcile`. Without the marker, a Next that names either skill does not make an `in-review` plan await repair.

A reader routes an `in-review` task awaiting repair as § *Reopening marker* routes a `done` task, reading the repair marker in place of the reopening marker.

## Revising a cited plan

Before a revision removes, defers, or renumbers a step, find each sibling citation of it. Such a citation names the revised task's folder, written `<folder> Step <n>`. The group folder is the revised task's parent, or the container's parent for a task inside `Backlog/`. Search the `**Depends on:**` lines of each sibling task's `plan.md` in the group folder and in its `Backlog/`, not its `Archive/`. Raise each citing step to the user under `../../CORE_RULES.md` § *Ask Before Assuming* before dependent writes. Name `plan-task` to update each citing plan. The revising run never edits another task's plan.

Keep each cited step's number stable. Insert a new step as `Step 3a` or `Step 3b` instead of shifting a cited step to another number.
